# Realism integration and evidence: round five

This round tightens planar placement and studies optional appearance changes without changing the established appearance defaults. It is an engineering study, not a claim that synthetic images match real captures or improve a detector.

## Implemented contract

- All 2D families drop a later target when its alpha support above the family threshold overlaps a previously accepted target in scene or output coordinates. Edges below that threshold may still overlap. Best-effort generation may retain fewer instances; require-count generation retries incomplete candidates. Renderer semantics v3.2 requires fresh generation pools while existing pools remain readable and exportable.
- Optional `scene.occlusion.visibility_policy: reject-visible-below-floor` rejects a candidate with an unlabeled, above-threshold visible fragment below its configured visibility floor. The legacy default preserves historical behavior; completely hidden targets may still be omitted.
- Optional local foreground context uses the unaltered projected background near each target to make bounded sRGB adjustments. Its independent per-object stream, alpha preservation, parameters and limitations are described in [SURFACE_RENDERING.md](SURFACE_RENDERING.md). Restrained and broad configurations are experimental and uncalibrated, not promoted defaults.
- The optional Blender harness drops later intersecting physical pad footprints, tracks survivor identities, and exports deliberate stacked overlap cases only as separate diagnostics. Panels sit on a flat z=0 ground plane; procedural bump affects shading rather than geometry. Evidence from actual renders is needed to assess visible contact artifacts.

## Evidence and reproducibility

The saved [2D context study](../experiments/foreground-context/README.md) compares paired baseline, restrained and broad results on development and reserved seeds, preserving geometry and annotation signatures. The [bounded L4 study](../experiments/blenderproc/studies/realism-round/README.md) uses saved 3D matrices, independent development and reserved scene intents, adaptive resource limits, verified artifacts and a conservative allocation ledger. Both use shared input assets across phases; these are synthetic combinations, not asset-independent or real-image holdouts. Visual inspection should consider unintended fragments, contact, color and texture changes, geometry, clipping, and readability rather than treat visual preference as detector validation.

The first Colab CPU 2D attempt stopped before generation after the 128 MB input upload returned HTTP 500; its runtime shut down and no paired evidence was produced.

The local 2D development attempt stopped at the existing host swap-out guard (35.4 MB additional swap-out, 8.18 GB minimum sampled available memory, about 1.60 GB peak process-tree RSS). It did not complete a paired comparison. The full test suite was finishing at the same time, so the system-wide swap activity cannot be attributed solely to the study. Do not use that partial run for throughput or quality conclusions, and do not automatically weaken the guard to rerun it.

The L4 development phase produced all 48 requested renders (20 baseline and 20 candidate, three wall probes, three contact diagnostics and two 1280-pixel probes). The original study status is **failed** because final postprocessing subtracted 640- and 1280-pixel images; it was not relabeled as successful. A verified recovered archive and independent re-analysis found 20/20 exact paired baseline/candidate masks. The baseline and candidate render commands took 16.33 and 13.42 seconds respectively, excluding installation, preparation, handoff and other cells; these small samples are not robust throughput estimates. A visual review selected baseline for reserved assessment: the candidate introduced narrow dark bars into some images, while both treatments showed limited visible contact shading. These judgments are synthetic, not real-image or detector validation. The originally failed archive remains the source of record; its recovery gate verifies the exact failure, all outputs and checksums before a reserved phase. The analysis now explicitly records different-sized images as incomparable instead of subtracting them. Recorded ordinary panel bottoms were within floating-point noise of z=0, with no visible clipping confirmed in the inspected closeup. No ordinary development placement was dropped for overlap, so this render sample does not exercise the collision rejection; targeted tests and the separate stacked diagnostic cover different parts of that contract.

The separately frozen L4 reserved phase completed 12 baseline and 12 exploratory-candidate images with 12/12 exact paired masks. Their render command times were 12.58 and 9.74 seconds respectively; setup, asset transfer, scene preparation and retrieval are excluded, and the short single pass is not a stable throughput benchmark. The selected baseline remains the primary configuration; reserved assets were not held out. Independent reserved-image review again identified narrow dark intrusions in candidate scenes 003–005 and limited visible pad contact shading; RGB alone does not establish whether the marks are geometry or shadows, or whether the pads clip or float. See the local standalone reports generated from the verified archives for full visual evidence.

After the rejected single-file upload, a checksum-verified chunked retry completed the Colab CPU 2D development comparison: 20 paired scenes retained identical geometry, annotations and mask signatures. Restrained context applied to 28 of 51 target instances; broad applied to 51 of 51. Independent visual review selected restrained for the reserved check: broad produced purple, nearly black or pale target colors in several cases and did not clearly resolve the pasted-on appearance. On this one ordered 1280² pass with two workers and one native thread each, generation-only rates were 0.188, 0.198 and 0.206 images/s for baseline, restrained and broad, respectively. Caching, order and noise make these rates unsuitable for claiming a treatment speedup; setup and transfer are excluded. The selection does not promote a production default or establish detector performance.

The separately frozen reserved 2D phase completed 12 paired scenes with identical geometry, annotation and mask signatures. Restrained was applied to 14 of 31 instances; broad to 31 of 31. Independent review again found restrained close to baseline while broad produced purple and washed-out targets in several scenes; none of the variants visibly resolves weak pad contact. A bright circular patch overlaps a target in reserved slot 9 across all three variants; it is not visibly caused by the new context treatment and warrants investigation as an existing scene or capture effect. Generation-only rates were 0.308, 0.303 and 0.298 images/s in the same fixed variant order on this different, small scene set. These are single-pass measurements, not evidence of a speed difference or transferable realism.

Across both 3D phases, the initial failed 2D upload, the successful 2D development retry and reserved 2D phase, local allocation-ledger start-to-confirmed-stop durations total approximately 20.55 minutes. All five named sessions report closed. Colab account balance was 158.72 units on the final check versus 158.97 before these attempts; balance changes may be delayed and are not a reliable per-phase charge measurement. Every rate above excludes at least some setup, transfer or handoff costs. The full test suite passed 539 tests; two independent visual reviews were qualitative and limited by thumbnail scale.

## Future work and boundaries

Real capture calibration, independent real-image evaluation and trained-model comparison remain open under issues #59, #61 and #64. More physically grounded lighting, material and surface interaction work remains under #62 and #63; the local color adjustment is not a physical relighting model and cannot remove baked shadows. Richer foreground/background assets, texture calibration and scene geometry require separate evidence and decisions. Keep 2D and 3D comparisons paired within each engine, record end-to-end costs, and avoid treating cross-host throughput or human preference as proof of model accuracy. BlenderProc remains an optional engineering harness rather than a required core backend.


A later exploratory haze diagnostic found that the volume bottom was coincident with the ground plane: three matched L4 scenes lost their ground texture even at low haze density. Moving the lower boundary to z=-2 m, leaving the top at z=16 m, restored the ground in the same three views; 9/9 paired geometric masks matched. This points to a coincident-surface problem, though the renderer internals were not instrumented. The fixed homogeneous-volume effect remains qualitative and is not calibrated atmospheric weather. The independent exploratory artifacts and matrices are retained under the task workspace’s ignored outputs; they do not establish real-world or detector performance.
