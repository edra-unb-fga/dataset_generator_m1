# Camera effects and robustness: evidence for a bounded study

This note concerns whole-composition capture effects and distinguishes physically motivated image formation, intentionally nonphysical robustness augmentation, and evidence about models. Recommendations below are hypotheses for this project's pads and gauges; neither attractive synthetic examples nor results in another dataset establish real-world detection or gauge-reading gains here.

## What is already available

The executable transform allowlist in `src/dataset_generator_m1/filters.py` and `docs/APPEARANCE_EFFECTS.md` includes final-stage Gaussian and motion blur, Gaussian or additive noise, brightness/contrast, gamma, color temperature and JPEG compression. The experimental `coherent-varied` appearance selects whole-image Gaussian noise at probability 0.15, normalized standard deviation 0.003–0.012 (about 0.8–3.1/255), motion blur at 0.20 with 3–5 pixel kernels, JPEG at 0.30 with quality 88–98 and brightness/contrast at 0.35. The corresponding manometro candidate reduces motion probability to 0.05 with a 3-pixel kernel. These settings are selected experimental inputs, not calibrated camera statistics; activation is not the same as perceptibility.

The current catalog does **not** expose a physical camera pipeline, shot/read noise, downscale, chromatic aberration or depth-dependent defocus as first-class transforms. Upstream documentation mentioning a newer transform does not imply it is present in the project's installed, allowlisted Albumentations 2.0.8. The previous `realistic-heavy` contains 10% final salt-and-pepper noise, 8% simulated flare and 8% rain; the coherent candidate does not. Previously tested 3-pixel forced motion visibly softened some very small dial marks. Preserve the existing unmodified profiles and the independent per-effect seed and trace contract.

## Research and interpretation

1. **Capture effects can help synthetic-to-real detection in a specific setting.** [Carlson et al., ECCVW 2018, *Modeling Camera Effects to Improve Visual Learning from Synthetic Data*](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content_ECCVW_2018/papers/11129/Carlson_Modeling_Camera_Effects_to_Improve_Visual_Learning_from_Synthetic_Data_ECCVW_2018_paper.pdf) combined clean synthetic driving images with versions augmented using sampled blur, chromatic aberration, exposure, noise and color. For Faster R-CNN car detection trained on 2,975 Virtual KITTI images and evaluated on a held-out KITTI set, AP50 rose from 54.60 to 61.88. Their ranges were tuned and several factors changed; this is not evidence that any individual parameter or the same gain transfers to our setup. The augmented training set also contained additional generated examples, so match training samples and budget when designing our comparison.
2. **Corruption robustness and clean accuracy need separate checks.** [Michaelis et al., *Benchmarking Robustness in Object Detection*](https://arxiv.org/abs/1907.07484) evaluated Faster R-CNN on synthetic corruption variants and natural condition changes. In their experiments mixing clean and stylized images improved corrupted performance more consistently than stylized-only training, which substantially hurt clean performance; it is not a guarantee for camera noise. [Lopes et al., *Patch Gaussian Augmentation*](https://arxiv.org/abs/1906.02611) reported RetinaNet COCO AP 33.2 baseline, 34.2 with patch Gaussian and 29.3 with a strong global Gaussian setting; the patch treatment is an optimization augmentation, not a physical sensor simulation. Avoid extrapolating the tested strong noise ranges to our defaults.
3. **Sensor noise is not uniformly distributed screen-space grain.** [Brooks et al., CVPR 2019, *Unprocessing Images for Learned Raw Denoising*](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content_CVPR_2019/papers/Brooks_Unprocessing_Images_for_Learned_Raw_Denoising_CVPR_2019_paper.pdf) model raw shot/read noise and camera processing for denoising. [Zamir et al., CVPR 2020, *CycleISP*](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content_CVPR_2020/html/Zamir_CycleISP_Real_Image_Restoration_via_Improved_Data_Synthesis_CVPR_2020_paper.html) discuss signal dependence and processing effects. Their downstream task is denoising rather than detection. Independent identically distributed Gaussian noise on a JPEG/sRGB image is a controlled robustness approximation, not calibrated physical raw noise. Upstream [Albumentations GaussNoise documentation](https://albumentations.ai/explore/transform/GaussNoise/docs/) documents normalized standard deviation and channel behavior; exact parameters must be verified against the installed version.
4. **Sensor and illumination effects interact.** [Kim et al., WACV 2026, *ImageNet-sES*](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/WACV2026/html/Kim_ImageNet-sES_A_First_Systematic_Study_of_Sensor-Environment_Simulation_Anchored_by_WACV_2026_paper.html) reports classification robustness benefits from simulated sensor/environment shifts anchored by real recaptures. This motivates coordinating darker exposures, increased gain/noise and potential blur, rather than applying strong noise uniformly. The paper's results are not direct pad-detection evidence or a ready calibration for this camera.

## Suggested experiments

- Start with full-resolution, seed-matched final-stage ablations using existing supported transforms: clean/coherent baseline; restrained brightness/white balance; mild and visibly stronger Gaussian noise; restrained JPEG compression; mild/strong motion blur. Compare each alone and in bounded combinations. Include small manometro dials and sharp targets; record transform activations and sampled parameters. Retain clean cases and restrict conspicuous or severe cases rather than applying every effect everywhere. These are tentative categories, not claimed frequencies.
- If a more realistic candidate is worth the additional code, prototype a **signal-dependent shot/read-like noise approximation** in a declared linear-light capture stage with explicit coefficients, conditioning its strength on an exposure/gain hypothesis. Compare it with existing sRGB Gaussian on identical scenes. Label it an approximation: real background photographs are already processed and may contain baked camera noise, so a second virtual ISP could double-apply effects. Do not call generated coefficients calibrated without real reference captures.
- Explore simulated resolution loss and defocus as distinct hypotheses; keep motion blur conditional on plausible motion/exposure. Treat vignetting, glare and chromatic aberration as lower-priority optional studies; avoid default circular synthetic hotspots and salt-and-pepper unless a specific stress use justifies them. Geometry-changing distortion requires transforming imagery and annotations consistently; it cannot be added as an appearance-only pixel effect.
- Use image-first paired galleries with full settings, crops and visible markings, but reserve judgments of utility for later training/evaluation: same generator seeds/assets/splits, class mapping, dataset size and training budget; multiple training seeds; clean plus corruption-specific and severity-stratified tests; independent real sessions when available; detection and gauge-reading results separately. Monitor accepted throughput, process-tree RSS and effect timings without confusing these with model performance.

A practical order, not an established universal camera pipeline, is shared scene illumination/composition, candidate optical blur, signal/exposure/camera effects, then encoding or compression; explicitly document any resize because its position changes noise and blur. Preserve full/visible masks and annotations for appearance-only stages, retain original metadata and no-capture variants, and avoid repeated JPEG encoding when comparing variants. Some captures may already contain noise, flare or baked shadows; sampling more of the same can create unrealistic double processing.
